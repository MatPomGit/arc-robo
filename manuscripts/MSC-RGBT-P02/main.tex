\begin{abstract}
Multispectral fusion depends on physically meaningful exposure timing and spatial registration, not merely frame-arrival order. Does verified exposure-time alignment and geometric registration improve held-out object detection over unsynchronized and single-modality baselines? The prospective design is as follows: Collect or reuse calibrated paired RGB/LWIR sequences with documented exposure clocks, common-time transforms and independently assessed alignment residuals. The primary outcome is Held-out detection recall at a fixed false-positive rate, with measured temporal skew and spatial registration error as explanatory variables. The manuscript defines a research question and an evaluation strategy; it does not report experimental estimates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Detailed scientific scope and novelty boundary}
The contribution concerns physically meaningful exposure-time alignment and geometric registration of paired RGB and long-wave infrared sensors. Arrival timestamps are not exposure timestamps. The experiment must distinguish fusion effects from detector confidence and sensor sampling. This study owns the effect of temporal and spatial registration, not robustness under missing modalities (P04) or calibrated predictive risk bounds (P08).

\section{Falsifiable hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1.} With fixed detection models and a matched false-alert operating point, measured exposure-time and geometric alignment improve held-out object-detection recall in dynamic scenes over unregistered fixed fusion.

\paragraph{H2.} At increasing temporal skew, valid cross-modal associations decrease, with a larger degradation in high-motion scenes than in static scenes.

\paragraph{H3.} An independently calibrated alignment-validity mask lowers mismatched cross-modal associations compared with accepting all fused pairs, without decreasing valid-pair coverage beyond a predefined margin.

\section{Operationalization and study population}
\subsection{Independent unit of analysis}
One physical capture sequence or independently recorded dynamic scene; multiple aligned frame pairs are repeated observations.

\subsection{Data, sensors and ground-truth requirements}
Paired sensor recordings with source-native geometry, original exposure timestamps, verified common-clock transformations, registered calibration targets, independent object annotations, field-of-view masks and checksummed acquisition metadata. Data and models will be versioned with checksums, licensing information, instrument parameters, selection rules and clock provenance. The held-out test cohort must be independent at the acquisition-session, scene or participant level rather than at the individual frame level.

\subsection{Primary and secondary endpoints}
The primary estimand is defined as follows: Held-out object-detection recall at a fixed false positives per labelled frame budget, evaluated on independent dynamic scenes with measured exposure and registration evidence.

The secondary measurements will be: Cross-modal association precision, spatial reprojection residuals, inter-exposure time skew, coverage of the valid overlap region and end-to-end latency. The exact numerator, denominator, outcome threshold and permitted exclusions must be fixed in the protocol before inferential analysis.

\section{Planned experiments and research sequence}
\subsection{A. Sensor timing and geometric calibration}
Identify native camera grids, thermal exposure mechanisms, shared clock or verified clock transform, intrinsics/extrinsics and overlap masks. Independently measure spatial residuals, timing offsets and uncertainties. Exclude unverifiable clock claims.

\subsection{B. Controlled misalignment replay}
Perturb exposure-skew and registration parameters in paired labelled sequences while keeping source frames and detectors fixed. Compare RGB-only, LWIR-only, fixed raw fusion and calibrated fusion at one frozen false-alarm operating point.

\subsection{C. Independent dynamic-scene evaluation}
Use capture-session-disjoint moving scenes with verified exposure timing and independent object labels. Stratify by object velocity, occlusion and observed clock error; report association correctness and recall alongside registration residuals.

\subsection{D. Causal error decomposition}
Hold detection scores constant and separately vary geometry, timing and overlap masks. Quantify whether any recall gain is due to time alignment, spatial validity or an unrelated confidence-threshold change.

\section{Comparative design and statistical inference}
Use paired per-sequence contrasts and mixed regression with temporal skew, geometric residual, object speed and their interactions. Report sensor/scene-stratified uncertainty and compare at matched false-positive points. Do not optimize calibration or fusion on final test scenes. When human participants or independent trajectories are needed, their number must follow a documented power simulation or precision calculation after a feasible pilot. Report uncertainty, negative results and sensitivity to failure strata. Thresholds, model configurations and exclusion criteria must be frozen before final testing.

\section{Evidence gates, reproducibility and ethical boundary}
No physical synchronization claim from arrival-time alignment or synthetic offsets. Require lawfully used independently labelled pairs, measured timing and calibration residuals before confirmatory testing. Each study phase will preserve source SHA or release, configuration, labels, independent oracle, random seeds, raw event timing, and an auditable trace from inputs to computed figures. Simulated evidence is distinct from measured hardware outcomes. Participant studies require prior ethical review and consent.
